# READING THE DASHBOARD

> Everything the page does for the person reading it. None of it needs explaining to use, but
> knowing it is there is the difference between reading a long document and navigating one.

---

## 1. Getting around

| | |
|---|---|
| **Sidebar** | Every document, grouped. Click one to open it; click the caret on the open one to show its sections. |
| **Section rail** | The sections of the open document, with the one you are reading marked. |
| **Previous / next** | At the foot of every document, in sidebar order. |
| **Top bar** | Appears once you scroll past the title, so you always know which document you are in. |
| `/` | Focus the search box. |
| `[` | Hide the sidebar, or bring it back. |
| `Esc` | Close the full-size view, clear the search, or release a pinned diagram. |

The address bar tracks where you are (`#/2-diagrams/why-elk`), so a link to a specific section of a
specific document can be pasted to someone else — and the back button works.

## 2. Search

Filters the sidebar as you type, matching both document titles and section headings. A document
with matching sections opens itself so you can see them. It is a navigator, not a full-text
search — it looks at the titles, not the prose.

## <a id="folding"></a>3. Folding sections

Click any heading to fold its section away; click again to open it. The arrow in the margin does
the same thing.

What is folded is remembered per document, so a long reference page stays folded down to the parts
you actually use. Following a link into a folded section unfolds it — and every section above it —
on the way.

Selecting text inside a heading does not fold it, so you can still copy a title.

## <a id="full-size"></a>4. Diagrams full size

Every diagram has a header: what kind of diagram it is — and its title, when the source gives one in
mermaid's front matter (`title:` between two `---` lines) — with its buttons on the right.

Click a diagram's border, or the **Expand** button in its header, to open it full size. Then:

- **Drag** to pan, **scroll** to zoom, or use the `−` / `+` buttons.
- **Hover and click still work** — see [Diagrams](./2.%20DIAGRAMS.md#hovering-and-pinning). This is
  the combination that makes a wall-sized flowchart usable: zoom to the region, then pin the box
  you are following.
- `Esc` or **Close** to come back.

A diagram opens at a size that fits the window, but never so small it stops being legible — a very
tall flowchart opens readable and is panned rather than shrunk to nothing.

Clicking a box or an arrow inside a diagram pins it rather than opening the full-size view; that is
why the Expand button exists.

### <a id="collapse"></a>4.1 Collapsing a diagram

**Collapse**, in the same header, folds a diagram down to the header alone: a white box the height
of a line, so a document with large diagrams can be read as text. A click anywhere on the box opens
it again. The page remembers which diagrams you collapsed, in your browser, per document — they stay
collapsed when you come back, and when the diagram's source is edited. A collapsed diagram is not
printed.

## <a id="comments"></a>5. Comments

Select any text in a document and a **Comment** button appears. The comment shows in the margin,
tied to the sentence it belongs to; hovering either the highlight or the card lights both, so the
link between the margin and the text is never a guess.

Some things worth knowing before relying on them:

- **They live in your browser, not in the repository.** Nobody else sees them, and they do not
  travel with the file. They are for reading a draft, not for reviewing one as a team.
- **They survive an edit.** A comment stores its quote plus a little context either side, so after
  the document is changed and rebuilt it finds its sentence again. If the sentence is gone, the
  comment is kept and marked *text not found* rather than thrown away.
- **They can be turned off** — for you, with the *Comments* switch in [Settings](#settings); for a project,
  with `"comments": false` in [the config](./3.%20CONFIGURATION.md#settings), which removes the
  feature from the page entirely.
- **A browser that refuses storage loses them** when the page closes. The page says so the first
  time it happens rather than pretending to have saved.

When the margin is too narrow for a readable card, the cards shrink to numbered dots that open on
click, rather than overlapping the text.

## <a id="reviews"></a>6. Reviews

Every section has a **Reviewed** button at the end of its heading, and the document has one in the
corner of its title card. Read a part, and if you agree with what it says, click it: the check turns
green, and the sidebar shows the same check beside the section and the document.

The marks roll up and down the document's outline:

- **Marking a chapter or the whole document** marks everything inside it. Clicking it again clears
  them all.
- **Marking the last open section of a chapter** marks the chapter, and the last open chapter marks
  the document. Until then, the title card counts how far you are: *3 of 9 sections*.
- **Clearing one section** opens its chapter and its document again.

A review is of the words, so it lasts as long as they do:

- **A section whose words change** opens again, and so do its chapter and its document. Hover its
  button to see when it was reviewed. Re-wrapping lines or changing only the formatting keeps it.
- **A section added later** is not reviewed, so it opens its chapter and its document too. A
  renamed section counts as a new one, and so does a document that is renamed or moved.

The marks belong to the project, not to your browser. They are kept in `docs.reviews.json`, next to
`docs.config.json`, one line per mark: commit it, and everyone sees the same reviews and git shows
who reviewed what. Every build shows the marks in the file, but only a page served by
`lowkey-doc --watch` can change them, since a page opened from its file cannot write anything. With
`--watch` running, a mark reaches every other open copy of the page, and so does a change made to
the file from outside, by a `git pull` for instance.

A project can remove reviews with `"reviews": false` in [the config](./3.%20CONFIGURATION.md#settings).

## <a id="settings"></a>7. Settings

**Settings**, at the foot of the sidebar, opens the page's settings in a dialog in the middle of the
page. The documents stay in view behind it, so every change shows on the page while you make it.
**Esc** or a click outside closes it.

- **Side margins** — the room from the edge of the page to the text, either side. *Automatic* goes
  back to the default: the text at its reading width, in the middle of the page.
- **Comments** — shows or hides your margin comments, with how many there are beside it. This one
  is yours alone: it is kept in your browser and never written to the project.

Every other setting belongs to the project, not to one reader. With `lowkey-doc --watch` running, a change is
saved to the project's `docs.config.json` (created if there is none) a moment after the slider
stops, `index.html` is rendered again with it, and any other open copy of the page reloads to
match. A page opened from its file only previews a change, and it is gone on reload: to keep it,
open the page through `--watch`, or set it in [the config](./3.%20CONFIGURATION.md#margins).

## 8. Printing

The sidebar is dropped, every folded section is opened, and the fold arrows, heading anchors and
review buttons are hidden. What prints is the document, not the interface.

## 9. What the page needs

Nothing. No server, no network, no install. Open `index.html` by double-clicking it.

If your browser refuses local storage — some do, on `file://` — the dashboard still works; it just
will not remember which document you had open, what you folded, or your comments.
